Time trackers only work if you remember them

Clockify and the rest are good at storing hours. The hard part is pressing start and stop at the right moments, all day, for every client.

  • You forget to start it

    You open the client's file, get absorbed, and the first hour never makes it onto the timesheet.

  • You forget to stop it

    The timer keeps running through lunch, a school run or the next client's call.

  • You rebuild the week on Friday

    From memory, calendar entries and browser history, and the total is never quite right.

How it works

  1. 1

    ActivityWatch records your day

    The free, open source ActivityWatch notes which apps and windows you use and when you step away. Everything stays on your computer.

  2. 2

    You say what belongs to each client

    Tick the apps, windows and websites from your last week that belong to each client. There are no rules to write, and anything that matches no client is never billed.

  3. 3

    It fills in your timesheet, the way you bill

    Hourly, flat fee or retainer, whether short breaks count, how to round and any monthly limit. Review a day and write it, or let it fill in yesterday every morning.

Built for people who bill clients

  • Billed the way you agreed

    Each client gets its own agreement: hourly with a rate and currency, a flat monthly fee, a retainer or not billed. Short breaks, calls, rounding and monthly limits are applied exactly.

  • Time away from the computer

    Add a call, meeting or site visit for a client in a few clicks. It counts as work, and your private note never reaches the timesheet.

  • Nothing written without you

    Every day is shown before it is saved. Time already in your timesheet is skipped, and your own entries are never changed or deleted.

  • Every morning, if you want

    Turn on Every morning and yesterday is filled in before you start work. You can review any day first or write it yourself instead.

Your screen history stays on your computer

Window titles hold client names, document names, email subjects and private chats, and sometimes things that should never leave your computer, like a password reset link or an API key in a terminal.

autohours reads them on your computer and sends none of them anywhere. Clockify or your spreadsheet only receives the client, your own description, the times and whether it is billable.

Why not just ask an AI?

  • Private. An AI would need your full screen history every day.
  • The same answer every time. This is billing, so the same day always gives the same entries, and you can see exactly why.
  • Runs on its own. No prompting, no subscription and no cost per run.

No account, no tracking, no cloud service. This page has no analytics either.

Who it is for

  • Contractors and freelancers who bill by the hour.
  • People with more than one client or job, each billed differently.
  • Anyone who forgets to track time and rebuilds the week from memory.

Who it is not for

  • Employers watching staff. It tracks your own time on your own computer.
  • Work that mostly happens away from a computer.
  • Invoicing. It fills in timesheets; your invoicing tool takes it from there.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

No. autohours and ActivityWatch are free and open source, and Clockify's free plan is enough. A spreadsheet works too.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows, used every day. The Mac version is built and checked automatically with every release but is still experimental, because no one has tried it on a real Mac yet. Linux is not supported. If you try it on a Mac, please say how it went.

What happens when I step away or my laptop sleeps?

That time is left out, unless your agreement with that client counts short breaks or calls.

Can I undo it?

Edit or delete the entries in Clockify or your spreadsheet. autohours never deletes anything, and time already there is skipped next time.

Does it use AI?

No. It only matches the apps, windows and websites you chose for each client, so the result is the same every time.

The download says it is from an unknown publisher

The downloads are not signed yet. On Windows choose More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, right-click the app and choose Open. The README has the details.
